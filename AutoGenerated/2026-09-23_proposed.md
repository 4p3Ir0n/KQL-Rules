## 1) ClickFix / ClearFake: malicious command pasted into the Windows Run dialog (RunMRU)

**Tactic:** Execution  
**ATT&CK:** T1204.004  
**Severity:** High  
**Purpose:** ClearFake, IClickFix and related "ClickFix" fake-CAPTCHA lures instruct the victim to paste an attacker-supplied command into Win+R. The pasted string is persisted in the Explorer RunMRU registry key, making it a high-fidelity, infrastructure-independent signal of successful social engineering.

```kusto
DeviceRegistryEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(1d)
| where RegistryKey has @"\Explorer\RunMRU"
| where isnotempty(RegistryValueData)
| where RegistryValueData has_any ("powershell","pwsh","mshta","curl","bitsadmin","certutil","msiexec","rundll32","wscript","cscript","conhost","forfiles","ftp ","\\\\","http://","https://","-enc","-e ","frombase64string","iex","invoke-expression","invoke-webrequest","iwr ","downloadstring","-w h","-windowstyle h","# \u2713","captcha","verification","not a robot","ray id")
| project Timestamp, DeviceId, DeviceName, InitiatingProcessAccountName, RegistryKey, RegistryValueName, RegistryValueData, InitiatingProcessFileName, ReportId
```

**Tuning notes:** Very low volume in most environments. Admins and developers may legitimately paste cmd/powershell one-liners; allowlist known IT accounts or specific benign strings (e.g. \\fileserver\share paths, "powershell" alone with no URL/encoding). Consider raising severity when RegistryValueData contains both a URL and an encoding/execution keyword.

## 2) ClickFix chain: explorer.exe spawning hidden/encoded LOLBin downloader

**Tactic:** Execution  
**ATT&CK:** T1059.001  
**Severity:** High  
**Purpose:** Detects the execution stage of ClickFix/ClearFake/EtherHiding lures where the user-pasted command is launched directly by the shell (explorer.exe) or the Run dialog, producing powershell/mshta/curl/msiexec children with hidden windows, remote URLs, or base64 payloads.

```kusto
DeviceProcessEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(1d)
| where InitiatingProcessFileName =~ "explorer.exe"
| where FileName in~ ("powershell.exe","pwsh.exe","mshta.exe","cmd.exe","curl.exe","msiexec.exe","rundll32.exe","wscript.exe","cscript.exe","bitsadmin.exe","certutil.exe","regsvr32.exe")
| where ProcessCommandLine has_any ("http://","https://","frombase64string","-enc","-encodedcommand","downloadstring","downloadfile","invoke-webrequest","iwr ","invoke-restmethod","irm ","-w h","-windowstyle hidden","-nop","-noprofile","bypass","iex","invoke-expression","start-process","curl ","mshta ","msxml2.xmlhttp","net.webclient")
| project Timestamp, DeviceId, DeviceName, AccountName, FileName, ProcessCommandLine, FolderPath, InitiatingProcessFileName, InitiatingProcessCommandLine, ProcessId, ReportId
```

**Tuning notes:** Users double-clicking .ps1/.hta/.js files also yields explorer.exe parents \u2013 review FolderPath of the payload. Allowlist software-deployment scripts and helpdesk tooling by command-line substring. Tighten by requiring both a URL and a hidden-window/encoding flag if volume is high.

## 3) ClearFake / EtherHiding: script interpreter retrieving payload stage from blockchain RPC or pastebin-style host

**Tactic:** CommandAndControl  
**ATT&CK:** T1219  
**Severity:** Medium  
**Purpose:** ClearFake's EtherHiding technique fetches the next-stage loader from smart-contract read calls against public Ethereum/BSC/Polygon RPC endpoints, or from throwaway paste/CDN hosts. Detects script hosts and LOLBins making those outbound requests regardless of rotating domains.

```kusto
DeviceNetworkEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(1d)
| where InitiatingProcessFileName in~ ("powershell.exe","pwsh.exe","mshta.exe","wscript.exe","cscript.exe","curl.exe","cmd.exe","rundll32.exe","msiexec.exe","certutil.exe","bitsadmin.exe")
| where RemoteUrl has_any ("binance","bsc-dataseed","infura","alchemy","ankr","polygon-rpc","cloudflare-eth","llamarpc","drpc.org","publicnode","quiknode","pastebin","paste.ee","rentry","textbin","transfer.sh","tmpfiles","bashupload","gofile.io","catbox.moe","workers.dev","r2.dev","trycloudflare.com")
| summarize Connections = count(), Urls = make_set(RemoteUrl, 10), Ports = make_set(RemotePort, 5), FirstSeen = min(Timestamp), LastSeen = max(Timestamp) by DeviceId, DeviceName, InitiatingProcessFileName, InitiatingProcessCommandLine, InitiatingProcessAccountName
| project FirstSeen, LastSeen, DeviceId, DeviceName, InitiatingProcessAccountName, InitiatingProcessFileName, InitiatingProcessCommandLine, Connections, Urls, Ports
```

**Tuning notes:** Legitimate crypto/dev workstations will hit RPC providers \u2013 scope out developer device groups or require the initiating process to be a script host launched by explorer.exe/office. Keyword list should be maintained as ClearFake rotates providers; workers.dev/trycloudflare are also used by benign SaaS.

## 4) macOS ClickFix (Macfinger/AMOS): shell one-liner piping remote content to an interpreter

**Tactic:** Execution  
**ATT&CK:** T1059.004  
**Severity:** High  
**Purpose:** The Macfinger/IClickFix macOS lures tell victims to paste a Terminal command that curls a script and pipes it straight to bash/sh/zsh, often base64-decoded, to drop Atomic (AMOS) stealer. Detects the curl|sh execution pattern and osascript credential prompts.

```kusto
DeviceProcessEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(1d)
| where InitiatingProcessFileName in~ ("bash","sh","zsh","dash","Terminal","iTerm2","osascript") or FileName in~ ("bash","sh","zsh","curl","osascript")
| where (ProcessCommandLine has_any ("curl","wget") and ProcessCommandLine has_any ("| bash","|bash","| sh","|sh","| zsh","|zsh","bash -c","sh -c","eval"))
   or (ProcessCommandLine has "base64" and ProcessCommandLine has_any ("-d","--decode") and ProcessCommandLine has_any ("| bash","|bash","| sh","|sh","eval"))
   or (FileName =~ "osascript" and ProcessCommandLine has_any ("display dialog","with hidden answer","administrator privileges"))
| project Timestamp, DeviceId, DeviceName, AccountName, FileName, ProcessCommandLine, FolderPath, InitiatingProcessFileName, InitiatingProcessCommandLine, ReportId
```

**Tuning notes:** Developer and DevOps macOS/Linux hosts legitimately use curl|bash installers (Homebrew, rustup, nvm). Allowlist by known installer domains present in the command line or by device group; treat commands that also contain xattr -c, chmod +x in /tmp, or osascript password prompts as high confidence.

## 5) AsyncRAT/Quasar beaconing: binary in user-writable path connecting to non-standard C2 port

**Tactic:** CommandAndControl  
**ATT&CK:** T1571  
**Severity:** High  
**Purpose:** AsyncRAT and its forks execute from AppData/Temp/ProgramData and beacon over TLS-ish raw sockets on characteristic high ports (6606/7707/8808/1177/2404/4449/5552/6666/8848). Detects the beacon behaviour rather than the listed ip:port IOCs.

```kusto
DeviceNetworkEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(1d)
| where ActionType in ("ConnectionSuccess","ConnectionAttempt")
| where tolower(InitiatingProcessFolderPath) has_any ("\\appdata\\roaming\\","\\appdata\\local\\temp\\","\\programdata\\","\\users\\public\\","\\windows\\temp\\")
| where RemotePort !in (80, 443, 53, 8080, 123, 445)
| where RemoteIPType == "Public"
| summarize Attempts = count(), DistinctDestinations = dcount(RemoteIP), Ports = make_set(RemotePort, 10), Destinations = make_set(strcat(RemoteIP, ":", RemotePort), 10), FirstSeen = min(Timestamp), LastSeen = max(Timestamp)
    by DeviceId, DeviceName, InitiatingProcessAccountName, InitiatingProcessFileName, InitiatingProcessFolderPath, InitiatingProcessSHA256
| where Attempts >= 5 or Ports has_any (6606, 7707, 8808, 1177, 2404, 4449, 5552, 6666, 8848)
| project FirstSeen, LastSeen, DeviceId, DeviceName, InitiatingProcessAccountName, InitiatingProcessFileName, InitiatingProcessFolderPath, InitiatingProcessSHA256, Attempts, DistinctDestinations, Ports, Destinations
```

**Tuning notes:** Game launchers, chat clients, VPN and backup agents installed per-user will surface; allowlist by InitiatingProcessSHA256 or signer after review. Lower the Attempts threshold for tighter detection, or join to DeviceFileEvents to require the binary was created in the last 7 days.
